    Full URLs and www-links are trusted as-is, so internal or non-public hostnames (like .local or .test) still come through. Bare mentions with no "http://" or "www." — plain text, log lines, email addresses — are checked against the full IANA list of ~1,400 top-level domains before being accepted, so stray text like "e.g." or a file named "archive.zip" isn't mistaken for a domain. Root-domain grouping uses a list of common multi-part suffixes (.co.uk, .com.au, .co.jp, etc.); it covers the vast majority of real-world domains but isn't a full public-suffix list.
